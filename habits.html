How Students Are Contributing to Excessive Paper Use

Students Use A LOT of Paper!

The table on the left shows the paper consumption of A4-sized sheets across various academic institutions that school students are part of, such as schools, tuitions, coaching centres, etc. It shows that school students used 3882 kilotons of A4 paper, which is equivalent to 94.3 million trees, a number that should be concerning for all of us.

Most Of It Is Assessments and Note-Taking

Assessments

Another significant contributor to students’ high paper consumption is the assessments and examinations they undertake as part of their academic curriculum. Schools and other educational institutions frequently conduct written tests and examinations, which contribute to the overall demand for paper. As illustrated in the graph, assessments alone account for 396.32 kilotons of paper consumption annually.

Note-Taking

The graph also highlights that the majority of students’ paper consumption is attributed to note-taking. In India, it is common for school students to rely on physical notebooks to take notes across the wide range of subjects they study. Note-taking alone accounts for 2,284.3 kilotons of paper consumed annually, making it a significant contributor to the overall paper consumption.

Paper Use Is Not Stopping!

The graph above illustrates the gradual increase in India’s paper consumption from 2005 to 2022, with paper consumption rising from 7.5 million tonnes to 21 million tonnes. This proves that excessive paper consumption is a growing issue.
