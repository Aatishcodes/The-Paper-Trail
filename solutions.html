How You Can Reduce Your Paper Use

Approaches to Reduce Your Paper Use

Take Notes on Your Devices

Begin taking notes on devices such as tablets and laptops! This drastically reduces paper usage as it eliminates the need for paper in note-taking entirely. There are several services, such as Google Docs, Microsoft Word, Notepad, etc., that you can use to replace physical notebooks.

Give Assessments Online

Online testing software such as Safe Exam Browser, Assessprep, IB Player, etc. do not require answer sheets, question papers, and “extra rough” sheets. The transition from paper assessments to online assessments should be smooth for most students, as a study by Cambridge revealed that only 18.2% of questions showcased a difference in difficulty between physical and online test formats, and those differences were evenly split (Gaskell, Judith. “Digital versus Paper Assessments: The Results.”).

Use Online Textbooks

It is common to find online PDFs of textbooks that schools of various boards across India use, such as CBSE, NCERT, IGCSE, ICSE, etc. The online versions directly replace the physical versions made of paper and can be found easily on websites like Scribd, IB Docs, etc.

Maximize Unused Paper

It is also important to maximise existing paper by using the back of unused documents/worksheets, using an old year’s notebook to continue making notes, printing double-sided, donating textbooks to younger peers, and more. As a rule of thumb, try to use paper only when absolutely necessary.

Eco-Friendly Alternatives to Paper

Bamboo Paper

Compared to tree paper, bamboo pulp can be processed with fewer chemicals and requires less land to create the same volume of fibre (for pulp). This makes it a more eco-friendly alternative to regular tree pulp paper.

Seed Paper

Seed paper is a compostable and biodegradable type of paper made from recycled pulp (from trees) mixed with seeds. Unlike tree pulp paper, it breaks down easily in soil and is usually free from synthetic dyes and excessive chemical coatings, making it a great environmentally friendly alternative to tree pulp paper.

Straw Paper

Straw paper is made from the leftover stalks of harvested wheat, rice, and barley. The leftover stalks are often burned, which releases large amounts of carbon emissions. Turning the stalks into paper reduces waste and prevents unnecessary carbon emissions.
